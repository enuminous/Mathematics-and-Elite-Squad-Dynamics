# The Denominator Has Been Promoted
## Every Department Gets Worse. The Minister Gets a Medal.

**Mathematics and Elite Squad Dynamics: Swarm Buzzkill for the Buzzards**  
**Dispatch 09 · Statistics / Institutional Satire · October 21, 2026**  
**Matthew Chenoweth Wright · Monolithic LLC**

*Original fictional satire. The ministry, officials, dialogue, motives, and awards are invented. The numerical example is constructed; the arithmetic is exact. No actual government or institution is accused, and no new mathematical discovery is claimed.*

### 1. A historic improvement in the font size

The Ministry of Successful Outcomes announced its finest year on Tuesday, shortly after confirming that both of its departments had become worse at their jobs. This apparent difficulty was resolved at cabinet level by printing the total in a larger font. Easy cases were being mishandled more often; difficult cases had become almost ceremonial. Nevertheless, the national success rate had climbed from forty-five to seventy-five percent, a triumph attributed to leadership, resilience, and a consultant who had discovered the SUM function. I attended as the journal’s accredited parasite, carrying a notebook and the unreasonable expectation that percentages should remain accountable to the things being counted.

Behind the lectern hung a banner:

**NUMBERS CANNOT LIE WITHOUT AUTHORIZATION.**

The minister stood beneath it wearing the expression of a man who had personally invented addition and was considering charging the public for subtraction.

“Our success rate,” he announced, “has improved by thirty percentage points.”

This was true.

“Which proves the success of my reforms.”

This was considerably more ambitious.

The Department of Easy Cases had previously resolved nine out of ten cases. It now resolved seventy-two out of ninety. Its director received a written reprimand for allowing the success rate to fall from ninety to eighty percent.

The Department of Difficult Cases had previously resolved thirty-six out of ninety. It now resolved three out of ten. Its director received an identical reprimand for falling from forty to thirty percent.

The minister, who supervised both departments, received a performance bonus.

The consultant described this as the emergence of excellence at a higher organizational level. He had billed separately for the word “emergence.”

### 2. The clerk introduces a hostile table

A junior clerk approached the lectern carrying the following table. She had attempted to submit it through the normal channels, but the normal channels required a positive conclusion in the subject line.

**Constructed counts: successes / all cases received in the stated category and period**

| Case category | Before | After |
|---|---:|---:|
| Easy | 9/10 = 90% | 72/90 = 80% |
| Difficult | 36/90 = 40% | 3/10 = 30% |
| **Overall** | **45/100 = 45%** | **75/100 = 75%** |

The definitions of “easy,” “difficult,” and “success” had remained unchanged. All cases were counted. The incoming workload had shifted from mostly difficult cases to mostly easy ones. The story supplies no evidence that the minister caused this shift.

“Who approved the categories?” the minister asked.

“You did.”

“Then why are they attacking me?”

The permanent secretary leaned forward to explain that a table could be perfectly accurate and still show a regrettable lack of judgment.

### 3. An exchange concerning national arithmetic

**CLERK:** Both category rates fell ten percentage points. The overall rate rose because easy cases went from ten percent of the workload to ninety percent.

**MINISTER:** Precisely. My leadership has made difficulty statistically less important.

**CLERK:** The records establish the change in workload. They do not establish that you caused it.

**MINISTER:** Leadership is the capacity to accept responsibility for favorable developments without obstructing them through excessive involvement.

**CLERK:** With the old workload proportions, the new rates give thirty-five percent, below the previous forty-five.

**MINISTER:** Then the old proportions were sabotaging us. Have them investigated.

**CLERK:** Proportions cannot be dismissed.

**MINISTER:** Everyone says that until we reorganize the denominator.

The permanent secretary produced a velvet cushion bearing the number one hundred.

“For distinguished service to the national total,” he announced.

The minister pinned the medal to the denominator.

The clerk checked the pin against the procurement register. It cost more than her annual training allowance.

### 4. A promotion with no numerically inferior candidates

The denominator accepted its decoration without comment. Its hundred constituents, having been counted, were not invited to speak.

Strictly speaking, the total denominator had not changed at all: one hundred cases before, one hundred after. The departmental denominators had exchanged sizes. This distinction appeared in the ceremony’s technical supplement, which was printed on the underside of the stage.

The minister praised the denominator’s loyalty. It had allowed him to tell the truth about the total while allowing everyone else to infer something the total did not establish.

This, he explained, was transparency.

The clerk was transferred to the Office of Unmeasured Contributions. Its staff were excluded from the ministry’s employee satisfaction survey on the grounds that their inclusion would introduce dissatisfaction.

I asked whether the journal could reproduce the table.

“Certainly,” said the minister. “We believe in open data.”

“And the category labels?”

He summoned security.

I returned to the editorial office, where I discovered that our readership figures had improved after we began counting people who opened the wrong browser tab. For several minutes I considered a commemorative issue.

The denominator’s medal was very handsome.

### 5. Mathematical appendix: what actually follows

**Established mathematics.** An overall success proportion is a weighted average of category success proportions. Aggregating over groups can reverse a comparison visible within every group; this is a form of Simpson’s paradox. Simpson’s 1951 paper is a historical primary source on the dangers of interpreting combined contingency tables. The example here is our own.

**Explicit toy-model assumptions.** There are two exhaustive, disjoint categories, with stable category and success definitions. Every case has one recorded binary outcome. All category counts are positive. We stipulate the table above; these are not observations from an actual ministry.

Let \(w_t\) be the fraction of easy cases in period \(t\), and let \(e_t\) and \(d_t\) be the corresponding category success proportions. Then

\[
P_t=w_t e_t+(1-w_t)d_t.
\]

For the constructed example,

\[
P_0=0.1(0.9)+0.9(0.4)=0.45,
\qquad
P_1=0.9(0.8)+0.1(0.3)=0.75.
\]

Thus \(e_1-e_0=d_1-d_0=-0.10\), while \(P_1-P_0=+0.30\). These changes are in proportions: minus ten and plus thirty **percentage points**, respectively.

Holding the original workload weights fixed gives the standardized comparison

\[
P_1^{\mathrm{fixed}}=0.1(0.8)+0.9(0.3)=0.35.
\]

This is a calculated reweighting, not an observed outcome or a prediction of what a staffing intervention would cause.

More generally, for the same fixed weight \(0<w<1\),

\[
P_1-P_0=w(e_1-e_0)+(1-w)(d_1-d_0)<0
\]

whenever both category changes are negative. Under these assumptions, both category proportions cannot fall while their average rises unless the weights change.

**Empirical boundary.** The counts alone do not establish changes in competence, causation, intent, or policy effectiveness. Even within-category rates can reflect differences in cases that the category labels fail to capture. The opening’s verdict that the departments became “worse at their jobs” is the fictional narrator’s shorthand for declining recorded success proportions, not a causal finding. Applying this example to a real institution requires actual data, stable definitions, relevant case characteristics, and a defensible comparison.

**Satirical boundary.** The medal, bonus, transfer, and ministerial explanations are inventions. No proof of this arithmetic validates their political interpretation. This dispatch reports no EFMW experiment, no Zoo execution, and no new physical law.
