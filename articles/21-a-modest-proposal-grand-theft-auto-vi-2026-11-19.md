# Dispatch 21 — A Modest Proposal for the Improvement of American Civilization Through Grand Theft Auto VI

*Being an earnest inquiry into the moral, economic, and technological benefits of spending thirteen years constructing a virtual Florida in which Americans may commit the crimes they are too exhausted to commit in the actual one.*

**Mathematics and Elite Squad Dynamics · November 19, 2026 · Satire**

*By our Special Correspondent for Applied Degeneracy, Digital Economics, and the Collapse of Western Civilization*

---

It is with considerable satisfaction that I announce the arrival of *Grand Theft Auto VI*, the latest triumph of American ingenuity, in which a civilization possessing the means to simulate entire metropolitan areas has elected to devote those means to stealing automobiles, shooting policemen, and purchasing trousers.

The enterprise has consumed thirteen years.

This is approximately the length of time required to educate a physician, establish a successful agricultural cooperative, or bring a moderately incompetent infrastructure project to its second environmental-impact assessment.

Rockstar Games, however, has employed this period to achieve something arguably more ambitious: **Florida, but with functioning collision detection.**

I consider this a remarkable accomplishment, particularly as the original Florida has thus far resisted such an improvement.

## I. Concerning the Present Scarcity of Meaningful Employment

There are, by some accounts, millions of young men in the United States who feel alienated from the economy, isolated from society, and disenchanted with the promises of capitalism.

Fortunately, an international consortium of computer programmers has found an answer.

They shall become car thieves.

Not actual car thieves, of course, for that would inconvenience the insurance industry and occasion considerable police attention. Rather, they shall purchase expensive computing equipment, subscribe to high-speed internet services, and engage in imaginary car theft under conditions carefully negotiated by corporate lawyers.

They shall experience liberty.

They shall experience rebellion.

They shall experience the exhilarating freedom of driving a stolen Lamborghini directly into a municipal fountain, after which they shall purchase a new pair of virtual sunglasses.

And what better emblem of the modern American spirit could there be than a man who has spent eighty dollars for permission to disobey laws that do not exist?

The criminal of the eighteenth century had at least the satisfaction of depriving a wealthy gentleman of his property.

The criminal of the twenty-first century must first purchase his own copy of the wealthy gentleman's property, agree to its terms of service, and occasionally download an update.

**Thus has capitalism accomplished what two centuries of law enforcement could not: it has made robbery profitable for the person being robbed.**

## II. A Wonderful New Species of Rebellion

I am particularly delighted by the philosophical sophistication of the enterprise.

The game is a satire of American consumer culture, financed by an enormously successful corporation, distributed through multinational technology platforms, and marketed through one of the most extravagant exercises in consumer anticipation ever devised.

It condemns materialism.

It offers an Ultimate Edition.

It mocks our obsession with brands.

It has a merchandise store.

It exposes the hollow promises of consumer capitalism.

It would be grateful if you subscribed.

I cannot imagine a more perfect arrangement. Indeed, I propose that the Smithsonian immediately establish a permanent exhibition dedicated to the subject, entitled:

**THE REVOLUTION WILL BE MONETIZED.**

Admission: $79.99.

Gift-shop access requires the Ultimate Edition.

## III. Of the Peculiar Customs of the People of Leonida

The fictional state of Leonida, we are assured, bears a striking resemblance to Florida.

I have examined this proposition with considerable care.

Leonida contains criminals, swamps, alligators, aspiring celebrities, extravagant wealth, conspiratorial thinking, astonishingly poor judgment, and people who regard the operation of motor vehicles as a competitive contact sport.

Florida contains these things also.

The principal distinction appears to be that in Leonida, a computer must labor intensely to produce such phenomena, whereas in Florida they arise spontaneously from the interaction of sunlight, alcohol, and municipal zoning regulations.

This suggests a fascinating scientific question.

If a simulation becomes sufficiently faithful to Florida, does it eventually develop its own homeowners' association?

And if so, can the association be defeated with a rocket launcher?

I submit this as a suitable subject for doctoral research.

The developers have constructed an enormous world of beaches, highways, neighborhoods, businesses, wildlife, weather, and intricate human behavior.

Here is an opportunity for unprecedented artistic achievement.

The human player may encounter the complexity of an entire civilization.

He may examine its inequalities, confront its moral contradictions, explore its architecture, and investigate the subtle interplay between personal freedom and institutional power.

Or he may hit a pedestrian with a golf cart.

I predict the latter will receive considerable scholarly attention.

## IV. Concerning Two Young Persons of Questionable Character

Our protagonists are Jason and Lucia, two young people seeking prosperity in a society whose economic opportunities have largely been monopolized by persons already possessing prosperity.

They are, in other words, entrepreneurs.

Their business model differs from that of Silicon Valley principally in that their thefts are represented directly on the screen.

A venture capitalist might observe that the pair have a compelling founding story, strong execution capability, excellent geographical market penetration, and an unusually aggressive customer-acquisition strategy.

Their principal deficiency is that they have not yet described their enterprise as artificial intelligence.

This may be remedied.

I propose a startup called **CrimeGPT**, whose purpose is to automate the commission of virtual felonies, thereby liberating the player to pursue higher-order activities such as reading quarterly shareholder reports.

Its seed round shall be $400 million.

Its product shall be an animated button labeled STEAL CAR.

Its founders shall describe it as transformational.

They will not be wrong.

## V. An Inquiry Into the Extraordinary Patience of the Gaming Public

Thirteen years.

Permit me to dwell upon this figure.

Thirteen years is an age at which a child may begin to develop independent moral reasoning.

A child born on the release date of *Grand Theft Auto V* is now old enough to ask why his father has spent the greater part of his adult life awaiting its sequel.

The father will explain that the new game possesses more realistic reflections, improved pedestrian behavior, and substantially greater fidelity in the depiction of automotive upholstery.

The child will naturally ask whether these advances have improved civilization.

The father will say that he does not know, but that the water looks absolutely incredible.

And it does.

We have reached the extraordinary stage of technical progress at which a simulated puddle may require more computational effort than the entire Apollo guidance computer, while serving the equally noble purpose of reflecting the undercarriage of a stolen pickup truck.

I shall not criticize this allocation of resources.

I merely suggest that NASA should have considered giving Neil Armstrong a wanted level.

## VI. The Internet, Having Finally Met Its Natural Enemy

Today, November 19, 2026, the game has arrived.

According to contemporary reporting, its download is approximately 212 gigabytes.

This is a remarkable quantity of information to transmit for the purpose of enabling a man to drive badly.

Internet providers have been obliged to prepare for the consequent demand.

Somewhere, at this very moment, a network engineer is gazing at a wall of flashing equipment and contemplating the civilization that has entrusted him with its infrastructure.

He has studied mathematics.

He has studied telecommunications.

He understands packet switching, congestion control, redundancy, and the statistical properties of network traffic.

He knows that the internet might facilitate universal education, international scientific cooperation, and the free exchange of human knowledge.

Instead, seven hundred thousand men are simultaneously attempting to download the same simulated strip club.

The engineer reaches for his coffee.

The router begins blinking.

And so, perhaps, does God.

## VII. A Few Words Concerning the Moral Improvement of Youth

Critics of the franchise have long argued that games of this nature encourage antisocial behavior.

This criticism, though doubtless sincere, seems to overlook the remarkable degree of social discipline required to participate in the modern gaming economy.

The average customer must:

- Obey the platform's rules.
- Maintain an account in good standing.
- Accept the publisher's licensing conditions.
- Preserve sufficient disk space.
- Remain connected to the relevant services where required.
- Refrain from certain prohibited forms of misconduct.

Having fulfilled these obligations, he may then enjoy the thrilling fantasy of being an individual who refuses to obey rules.

Thus, the modern criminal imagination is now cultivated in a carefully regulated commercial environment.

It is as though Robin Hood had been required to enroll in a loyalty program before entering Sherwood Forest.

There are rewards.

There are incentives.

There may be exclusive seasonal benefits.

The outlaw has become an account holder.

The revolution has a customer-support department.

## VIII. The Great Philosophical Discovery

There is, however, one feature of this phenomenon that troubles me deeply.

For decades, artists, philosophers, educators, and political reformers have struggled to persuade the public to examine the absurdities of modern American life.

They have written books.

They have produced documentaries.

They have organized demonstrations.

They have delivered lectures upon the moral bankruptcy of consumerism, the corrupting influence of wealth, the theatrical nature of celebrity, and the increasing difficulty of distinguishing public life from commercial entertainment.

Most have been ignored.

Rockstar Games has discovered a superior method.

It has constructed a colossal satire of the very same society, placed a stolen automobile in the foreground, and sold tickets to the experience.

Millions have arrived.

This represents a devastating indictment of the literary profession.

Jonathan Swift spent years fashioning arguments of exquisite irony and moral precision.

He might have achieved vastly greater commercial success had he included a minimap and a button for punching horses.

Indeed, *A Modest Proposal* itself might have enjoyed an altogether different reception under the title:

**GRAND THEFT IRELAND: THE POTATO HEIST.**

Downloadable content: *The Landlord Expansion.*

## IX. My Own Modest Proposal

Having considered the matter, I believe the franchise has not yet gone far enough.

Its developers should cease representing American society through satire and instead reproduce the institutions of American life with absolute fidelity.

I therefore recommend the following features for *Grand Theft Auto VII*.

The player shall begin with $47,000 in student debt.

His principal antagonist shall be a health-insurance company.

The wanted-level system shall be replaced by a credit score.

Successful criminal enterprises shall obtain venture financing, become too large to fail, and acquire their competitors.

The final mission shall involve navigating a customer-service telephone tree.

A player wishing to purchase property shall be required to demonstrate three years of stable income and provide documentation of previous rental history.

Police pursuit shall be suspended whenever the player enters a sufficiently expensive neighborhood.

The protagonist's automobile shall depreciate immediately upon purchase.

The player shall be unable to progress to the next mission until a state agency processes his application.

And the ultimate achievement, accessible only after approximately 400 hours of play, shall be called:

**HOMEOWNERSHIP.**

Upon unlocking it, the player shall receive a notification that his property taxes have increased.

This, I submit, would be a truly adult video game.

## X. A Final Consideration of the Human Condition

The genius of *Grand Theft Auto* is not that it allows its players to escape the world.

It is that it persuades them to purchase a second world that is even more ridiculous than the first, while congratulating themselves on having recognized the ridiculousness.

There is something almost tender about this arrangement.

We have built machines capable of calculating the motions of galaxies, modeling molecular interactions, and examining the most delicate structures of mathematical logic.

We connect them across a planetary communications network whose existence would have seemed supernatural to nearly every human being who ever lived.

And what do we do?

We simulate Florida.

Not because we are obliged to.

Not because Florida is in danger of extinction.

Not because the real Florida has become inaccessible, although this possibility cannot be entirely dismissed.

We do it because the simulated Florida permits us to commit atrocities without consequence, acquire wealth without labor, and inhabit a world where the moral bankruptcy of society is at least accompanied by an excellent soundtrack.

And we call it entertainment.

I cannot say whether this represents the pinnacle of human civilization or the moment at which civilization quietly excused itself from the room.

But I am inclined to believe that future archaeologists, examining the surviving data centers of our age, will conclude that we worshipped a peculiar deity called Rockstar, who appeared once every thirteen years, demanded considerable tribute, and rewarded the faithful with increasingly realistic representations of asphalt.

They will marvel at our ingenuity.

They will mourn our priorities.

And somewhere in the ruins, a server will still be attempting to verify a license.

---

## Editorial conclusion

It is difficult to remain entirely pessimistic.

For all its absurdities, humanity has finally achieved something philosophers have sought since antiquity:

**A world in which crime pays, death is temporary, and the laws of physics can be defeated by driving a motorcycle up a staircase.**

The price of admission is reasonable, provided one does not count the console, the broadband service, the additional storage, the electricity, the optional premium benefits, and the thirteen years of one's mortal existence spent waiting.

We may therefore proclaim with confidence that the future has arrived.

It is beautiful.

It is grotesque.

It is available for PlayStation 5 and Xbox Series X|S.

And the internet is blinking.

*God help us all.*
