## I. THE EQUATIONS HAVE ESCAPED THE LABORATORY

At 3:17 in the morning, the physicist discovered that the United States government had become an unstable particle accelerator.

This was not a metaphor he particularly wanted to investigate.

He had other work.

There were gravitational equations to examine, a research observatory called StorySpace to maintain, and a collection of experimental animals known collectively as the EFMW Zoo demanding that the word *UNRESOLVED* be treated with something approaching religious seriousness.

But Washington had produced another anomaly.

Eight articles of impeachment.

One secretary of defense.

A president publicly defending him.

A Republican congressman insisting that Congress perform its constitutional function.

And approximately forty thousand political commentators behaving as though the laws of thermodynamics had been repealed by executive order.

The physicist turned on the coffee maker.

The coffee maker made a sound like a small aircraft attempting to escape a federal subpoena.

He opened the Monolithic control console.

**EXPERIMENT: HGS-008 — INSTITUTIONAL STRESS UNDER PARTISAN LOAD**

**OBJECTIVE:** Determine what happens when eight impeachment articles collide with congressional procedure.

**KNOWN INPUTS:**

- Articles introduced: 8.
- Articles adjudicated: 0.
- House procedural motion: pending at the story's time of writing.
- Presidential support for Hegseth: publicly affirmed.
- Senate conviction threshold: two-thirds of members present.
- Public certainty that everyone already knows the outcome: extremely high.

The physicist examined the data.

"Jesus Christ," he said.

The machine answered:

**PLEASE DO NOT CONFUSE THE NUMBER OF ALLEGATIONS WITH THE NUMBER OF PROVEN OFFENSES.**

At last, an American institution capable of distinguishing arithmetic from evidence.

## II. THE MASSIE PARTICLE

Representative Thomas Massie had introduced the eight articles on November 9, alleging violations involving the Iran war, civilian protection, maritime operations, Venezuela, Yemen, and retaliation against Senator Mark Kelly.

These were serious allegations.

They were also allegations.

The distinction was essential, because the political system had developed a chronic inability to distinguish a charge, a press release, a committee hearing, a procedural vote, an impeachment, a Senate conviction, and a cable-news graphic containing the word **BREAKING**.

The physicist designated Massie **Particle M**.

Particle M had entered the chamber from a direction the party leadership appeared not to have anticipated.

This was particularly inconvenient because the House was already filled with other particles desperately trying to occupy the same limited electoral space.

Massie was not behaving according to the convenient geometry of a conventional partisan attack.

He was a Republican accusing a Republican administration's defense secretary of impeachable misconduct.

This produced what the imaginary simulation called **CROSS-PARTY INTERFERENCE**.

The physicist entered the initial state:

\[
|\Psi_{\mathrm{House}}\rangle =
\alpha|\mathrm{Table}\rangle+
\beta|\mathrm{Refer}\rangle+
\gamma|\mathrm{Consider}\rangle.
\]

The coefficients were deliberately left unspecified.

There were no reliable amplitudes.

There was no calibrated dataset.

And the physicist had already learned that assigning imaginary probabilities to a process one did not understand merely produced a numerically decorated form of ignorance.

The machine issued a warning:

**QUANTUM NOTATION DETECTED. THIS IS A METAPHOR, NOT A PHYSICAL WAVE FUNCTION.**

"Fine," said the physicist. "But it's a damn good metaphor."

## III. THE HEGSETH FIELD

The defense secretary appeared at a Pentagon briefing and rejected the impeachment effort.

The president continued to defend him.

The House Republican leadership opposed the move.

Meanwhile, Massie's allegations sat on the congressional docket like eight unexploded constitutional questions.

There are several things a government can do when confronted with allegations.

It can investigate them.

It can dispute them.

It can produce evidence.

It can argue about the applicable law.

Or it can generate enough political noise to make the entire issue resemble a television set falling down a staircase.

The physicist decided to measure the noise.

He introduced a fictional quantity:

\[
\mathcal{N} =
\frac{\text{Public confidence in the conclusion}}
{\text{Independently tested supporting evidence}+\varepsilon}.
\]

He called it the **Narrative Inflation Index**.

He ran it against imaginary press conferences.

The result exceeded the computational capacity of the room.

The printer caught fire.

A congressional consultant appeared on the screen and announced that the fire proved the administration's critics were acting irresponsibly.

Another consultant announced that it proved the secretary should be removed immediately.

Neither consultant had inspected the printer.

**NARRATIVE INFLATION INDEX: SATURATED.**

**EMPIRICAL CONCLUSION: NONE.**

The physicist extinguished the flames with a copy of the House rules.

This was the first documented occasion in his fictional laboratory on which congressional procedure had been useful for putting something out.

## IV. THE WAR POWERS SINGULARITY

Here the comedy became genuinely dangerous.

Three impeachment articles concerned the war with Iran and the War Powers Resolution.

One alleged the administration began hostilities without the necessary authorization.

Others challenged the continuation of hostilities, congressional withdrawal instructions, and statutory deadlines.

The physicist examined the congressional record.

Both chambers had approved a concurrent resolution calling for withdrawal from hostilities with Iran.

This was not imaginary.

But the legal machinery underneath it was a different matter.

The War Powers Resolution contains a mechanism involving a concurrent resolution, and the constitutional validity of using that mechanism to bind the executive is seriously disputed following the Supreme Court's decision in *INS v. Chadha*.

Suddenly there were two separate quantities:

**Congressional opposition to military action.**

And:

**The legally enforceable effect of the particular resolution Congress adopted.**

The physicist drew two circles on the laboratory wall.

They overlapped.

They were not identical.

He summoned the Zoo.

A tortoise emerged from a containment chamber wearing tiny reading glasses.

"Has Congress expressed opposition?" asked the tortoise.

"Yes."

"Has every legal question about that expression been resolved?"

"No."

"Does the dispute automatically establish criminal conduct by the secretary?"

"No."

"Does executive disagreement automatically dispose of every constitutional objection?"

"Also no."

The tortoise nodded solemnly.

**UNRESOLVED.**

The physicist wept with gratitude.

He had spent years deriving mathematical structures and discovered that the most difficult operation in Washington was distinguishing two related propositions.

## V. EIGHT ARTICLES ENTER, ONE PROCEDURAL MOTION LEAVES

The House was preparing to decide what to do with the impeachment resolution.

It could table it.

It could refer it.

Or it could permit consideration to proceed toward votes on the articles.

These were different institutional actions, but in political television they were frequently compressed into the same dramatic noun:

**IMPEACHMENT.**

The physicist introduced a procedural conservation law:

\[
\text{Motion to table} \neq \text{Vote to impeach}.
\]

And another:

\[
\text{House impeachment} \neq \text{Senate conviction}.
\]

The statements were so elementary that the software flagged them as suspicious.

"No one could possibly be confused about this," the computer said.

The physicist turned on the news.

Within eleven seconds, the computer apologized.

He opened a new file:

**AMERICA-001 — FAILURE TO DISTINGUISH PROCEDURAL STATES.**

The experiment had now crossed from theoretical physics into an obscure and much more terrifying discipline:

Elementary civics.

## VII. THE SENATE: WHERE ARTICLES GO TO DISCOVER SUPERMAJORITY MATHEMATICS

The physicist now turned toward the upper chamber.

The Senate was a peculiar device.

It could transform simple legislative questions into prolonged negotiations over procedure, scheduling, constitutional interpretation, and the meaning of the word *shall*.

Under the Constitution, conviction after impeachment requires two-thirds of senators present.

If all one hundred senators participate, that means sixty-seven votes.

This figure was not a prediction.

It was arithmetic.

The distinction pleased the Zoo enormously.

The physicist constructed a new model:

**STAGE A:** House procedural disposition.

**STAGE B:** House consideration and votes on articles, if permitted.

**STAGE C:** Transmission to the Senate, if any article is approved.

**STAGE D:** Senate procedural decisions and potential trial.

**STAGE E:** Acquittal or conviction, if the Senate reaches a final judgment.

Every stage required a separate event.

Every event required an actual vote or independently documented decision.

The physicist placed a large sign above the control console:

**DO NOT TELEPORT FROM STAGE A TO STAGE E.**

A Washington television producer called.

"Can you summarize the whole thing in six words?"

"Congress has not finished deciding anything."

The producer hung up.

The model had performed beautifully.

The ratings were catastrophic.

## VIII. THE STORYSPACE CONTAMINATION EVENT

The real danger arrived when the physicist attempted to forecast the headlines.

He found that every political faction had already written its ending.

One story described vindication.

Another described constitutional accountability.

A third described institutional sabotage.

A fourth described an inevitable Senate outcome.

Each story was exceptionally coherent.

Each could be made to explain almost any intermediate development.

A procedural defeat became proof that the administration was protected.

A procedural victory became proof that removal was inevitable.

A delay became a secret strategy.

A disagreement became evidence of a conspiracy.

The physicist recognized the pattern.

It was **StorySpace**.

The brain, the political consultant, and the television producer were performing the same essential operation:

Take a signal.

Supply a story.

Experience the story as though it had arrived with the signal.

He opened the StorySpace observatory and entered:

**SIGNAL:** Eight articles filed. House action approaching.

**STORY:** The final outcome is now inevitable.

**EVIDENCE:** No final House or Senate result recorded at this story's cutoff.

The machine returned:

The physicist stared at the screen.

"That," he said, "is the entire goddamn problem."

## XI. THE PHYSICIST MEETS THE CONSTITUTION

By now the coffee was cold.

The sky over California was beginning to lighten.

The physicist looked at the eight articles one last time.

The matters under dispute were not merely theatrical. They concerned war powers, civilian deaths, the use of military force, constitutional safeguards, and whether a senior executive official had acted within lawful authority.

Real lives, not invented tensors, were implicated in the underlying events.

This was the point at which the joke had to stop pretending that the problem was only comedy.

There were allegations that deserved evidence.

There were defenses that deserved to be heard.

There were constitutional questions that could not be resolved with a slogan.

And there were procedures established precisely so that a country would not have to substitute whatever story felt most satisfying for a decision grounded in an institutional record.

The physicist shut down the headline generator.

He left the evidence console running.
