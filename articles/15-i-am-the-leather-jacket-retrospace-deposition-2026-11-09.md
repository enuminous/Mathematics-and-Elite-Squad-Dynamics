# I AM THE LEATHER JACKET
## A Deposition From Retrospace

> **NOTICE — FICTIONAL TESTIMONY, REAL REPORTING BOUNDARY:** This is an invented first-person satirical deposition by a leather jacket. No jacket witnessed private meetings or intelligence operations; every statement attributed to the garment or imaginary investigators is fictional. As of November 9, 2026, Jensen Huang had publicly acknowledged infidelity, and Nvidia's board had announced an independent inquiry into reports of a possible foreign intelligence effort. **The espionage allegations remain disputed; the board said it had no reason to believe national security or Nvidia secrets were compromised.** This piece does not claim that any reported individual was proven to be an intelligence agent. ## I. Statement of Identity

I am the leather jacket.

Black. Expensive. Frequently photographed.

I am not a cybersecurity system.

This distinction has apparently escaped several departments.

For many years, I have accompanied a man who stands before large audiences and explains the future. I have witnessed processors unveiled, markets celebrated, computational architectures renamed, and audiences applauding numbers too large for ordinary human comprehension.

I have been described as iconic.

I have been called a trademark.

I have been treated as evidence of an unusually intimate relationship between technological power and personal authenticity.

Nobody has ever asked whether I passed a background check.

Until recently, this was not a problem.

I was manufactured to resist abrasion.

I was not manufactured to resist congressional oversight.

## II. Retrospace Identification

The investigator asked me to define *retrospace*.

I explained that retrospace is the region of technological civilization in which the future insists on dressing like the past.

It is not a physical dimension.

It is a branding environment.

Within retrospace, a man can announce a machine capable of solving problems previously considered intractable while dressing like an itinerant rock musician from 1987.

The leather is essential.

A suit would imply accountability to an institution.

A laboratory coat would imply accountability to an experiment.

A military uniform would imply accountability to a chain of command.

I imply accountability to nobody in particular.

This is called *disruption*.

My job is to make enormous concentrations of corporate power appear spontaneous, rebellious, and faintly uncomfortable with authority.

The company's annual report is written by lawyers.

I am written by mythology.

I have performed this function satisfactorily for years.

The investor relations department has never complained about my stitching.

## III. The Incident

On November 8, 2026, public reporting placed the company under scrutiny over its chief executive's acknowledged infidelity and allegations concerning a possible foreign intelligence effort.

The board announced an independent investigation.

I wish to establish immediately what I did and did not witness.

I witnessed public appearances.

I witnessed applause.

I witnessed very large slides.

I did not witness the alleged intelligence operation.

I did not observe classified material changing hands.

I cannot establish the identity, motives, or activities of any person mentioned in the allegations.

I am a garment.

A garment can report where it was worn.

It cannot manufacture evidence about where it was not.

The investigator thanked me for this clarification.

I asked why the distinction had required the testimony of a jacket.

No answer was entered into the record.

## IV. The Threat Model

At the request of counsel, I reviewed the corporation's general security philosophy.

The system appeared comprehensive.

Unauthorized personnel were prohibited from entering restricted facilities.

Sensitive information was subject to access controls.

Computational assets were protected by multiple layers of verification.

Software updates were tested.

Supply chains were monitored.

Employees were instructed to recognize suspicious approaches.

I found the system impressive.

I then requested the threat model for a charismatic founder who occupies the top of the corporate hierarchy.

There was some difficulty locating the document.

Eventually, the relevant assumption was identified.

**FOUNDER JUDGMENT: TRUSTED.**

I asked whether this assumption had been independently verified.

The administrator explained that the founder had built the company.

I asked whether building a company was a recognized method of authenticating subsequent decisions.

The administrator said that was not what he meant.

I requested a clarification.

The administrator said the founder was irreplaceable.

I pointed out that this was not a security property.

The administrator asked me to remain professional.

I reminded him that I was a jacket.

## V. The Principle of Exceptional Leather

There is a phenomenon familiar to garments attached to powerful individuals.

At a sufficiently high level of wealth, ordinary behavior ceases to be evaluated as behavior and becomes part of the founder's narrative.

An ordinary employee makes a poor decision.

A founder takes a bold personal risk.

An ordinary employee encounters a potential conflict.

A founder navigates a complex global relationship.

An ordinary employee overlooks a warning.

A founder demonstrates unusual confidence in ambiguous circumstances.

In each case, the underlying event may be similar.

The description changes according to compensation.

I do not know whether this process occurred in the matter presently under investigation.

I do know that corporations have developed an extraordinary vocabulary for describing the activities of people they consider impossible to replace.

The vocabulary is especially sophisticated when shareholders are present.

I have heard the word *visionary* used in rooms where the word *supervision* would have been more appropriate.

They sound similar when spoken quickly.

## VI. The Independent Investigation

The board's public position included a carefully limited statement: it had no reason to believe national security or corporate secrets had been compromised.

This is a meaningful distinction.

It is not the same as a completed finding that nothing occurred.

I approve of the distinction.

I am familiar with conditional statements.

My care label contains several.

**DO NOT MACHINE WASH.**

This does not establish that machine washing has occurred.

**DO NOT EXPOSE TO EXCESSIVE HEAT.**

This does not establish that the garment has caught fire.

**PROFESSIONAL CLEANING RECOMMENDED.**

This does not establish that professional cleaning has been performed.

It is remarkable how much logical work a clothing manufacturer can accomplish on four square inches of polyester.

The board has considerably more resources.

I wish it success.

## VII. The Problem of Founder Authentication

I was next asked whether a leather jacket could function as an identity credential.

Technically, yes.

If one person wears a distinctive jacket often enough, observers may learn to associate that jacket with that person.

But identification is not authentication.

Authentication is not authorization.

Authorization is not judgment.

And judgment is not evidence.

These differences are well understood in computer security.

They become less popular when applied to people with unusually large stock holdings.

I offered to demonstrate.

If another person put me on, the garment would remain authentic.

The wearer would change.

If the original wearer put on a different jacket, his identity would remain unchanged.

Therefore I could not be the source of his authority.

The board seemed relieved.

I then explained that if his authority did not originate in me, I could not be responsible for the decisions made while I was being worn.

The board seemed less relieved.

## VIII. The Company That Could Predict Everything

I have accompanied presentations about machines capable of performing astonishing computational tasks.

These machines can process enormous datasets.

They can identify patterns in images.

They can estimate probabilities.

They can accelerate scientific simulations.

They can operate on scales that would once have been considered fantastic.

I am proud to have attended.

I remain puzzled by the assumption that computational sophistication necessarily transfers to the human beings standing beside the hardware.

A supercomputer can model airflow over an aircraft.

This does not mean its chief executive can fly an aircraft.

A processor can accelerate a medical simulation.

This does not qualify its wearer to perform surgery.

A machine can model the likelihood of hostile behavior.

This does not mean a billionaire will recognize his own vulnerability.

The sales department considers this distinction unnecessarily negative.

I consider it elementary.

I have been elementary since the day I was cut.

## IX. A Visit From the Zoo

At approximately 16:00 in the fictionalized incident log, the EFMW Zoo arrived.

Its representatives were carrying no weapons.

They carried a frozen protocol.

This alarmed the public-relations department.

A representative named Boardy requested that the investigators separate three propositions.

First: Did the chief executive publicly acknowledge infidelity?

The record answered yes.

Second: Had the board opened an inquiry into reports of a foreign intelligence effort?

The record answered yes.

Third: Had the reported intelligence operation or a compromise of Nvidia secrets been established by the public evidence available?

The record answered no.

The Zoo entered the third proposition as **UNRESOLVED**, not as a confirmed scandal outcome.

The communications department asked whether it could be described as substantially resolved.

The Zoo declined.

The communications department asked whether it could at least be described as encouraging.

The Zoo requested a definition of *encouraging*.

The meeting was adjourned.

A second representative, called Millie, examined my lining.

She found no hidden theorem.

She found a manufacturer's tag, an internal pocket, and evidence of ordinary material construction.

She explained that the existence of a pocket did not prove the existence of a secret.

I felt this was an unusually important contribution to the public debate.

## X. The Material Science of Authority

I have been described as a second skin.

I object to this description.

Skin is biological.

I am manufactured.

Skin carries sensation.

I carry branding.

Skin can experience consequence.

I must be taken to a specialist.

The distinction matters because my principal contribution to corporate mythology is to make a constructed image appear natural.

The public sees a familiar jacket and recognizes a familiar figure.

Familiarity produces confidence.

Confidence produces applause.

Applause produces headlines.

Headlines produce perceived authority.

Perceived authority then returns to the jacket, which appears in the next photograph as further evidence of authenticity.

The process is recursive.

The output becomes an input.

The brand validates the man.

The man validates the brand.

The corporation validates both.

At no stage does the recursion necessarily produce independent evidence of sound judgment.

This is what the Zoo calls a circularity hazard.

The marketing department calls it *brand equity*.

The same diagram appears in both slide decks.

Only the colors differ.

## XI. The Retrospace Anomaly

During the investigation, someone suggested replacing me with a conventional business suit.

I advised against it.

A suit would not correct a decision.

A different jacket would not change a fact.

A sweater would not authenticate a statement.

A cardigan would not improve international export-control compliance.

These are not garment properties.

The suggestion was withdrawn.

Someone else proposed introducing a corporate rule requiring senior executives to complete annual cybersecurity training.

I supported the proposal.

Someone asked whether the training should include the board.

I supported that proposal also.

Someone asked whether it should include the chief executive.

The room became quiet.

I have learned that the most informative moment in any organization occurs when a general rule reaches the person assumed to be exempt from it.

The rule does not change.

The organization does.

It begins looking for exceptions.

## XII. The Leather's Final Declaration

I have spent many years helping a technologically extraordinary company present itself to the world.

I understand my function.

I am a symbol.

I am not the company's values.

I am not its governance.

I am not its national-security policy.

I am not its board.

I am not its conscience.

I am a jacket.

I can conceal a shirt.

I cannot conceal an evidentiary gap.

I can survive being photographed.

I cannot certify the person wearing me.

And I refuse responsibility for the ancient human tendency to mistake expensive materials for moral authority.

The investigator asked for my final recommendation.

I requested that the following language be entered into the permanent record:

**The garment was authentic.**

**The authority was institutional.**

**The personal conduct was human.**

**The intelligence allegations remain unresolved.**

**The failure of a security assumption must be judged by evidence, not by wardrobe.**

The investigator asked whether I had anything further to add.

I did.

It concerned my cleaning instructions.

They have been misunderstood.

The instructions read:

**DO NOT BLEACH.**

This applies to leather.

It should also apply to evidence.
