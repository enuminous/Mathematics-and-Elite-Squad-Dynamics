# Dispatch 22 — Fear and Loathing in the Brady Briefing Room

*Stephen Miller, the greatest two minutes in American history, and the unfortunate persistence of arithmetic.*

**Mathematics and Elite Squad Dynamics · November 21, 2026 · Gonzo political satire / fact-check commentary**

**Editorial boundary:** The November 20, 2026 remarks are real and linked below. Satirical imagery, rhetorical questions, and imagined explanations are commentary, not reported conversations or firsthand observations. The claims below distinguish official data, competing interpretations, and unresolved verification questions. No speaker's psychological state is asserted.

---

There are moments in American political history when the machinery of government produces something so magnificently absurd that satire becomes redundant. The facts have already put on a rubber nose, climbed into the clown car, and driven through the front doors of the White House.

Friday was one of those moments.

Donald Trump called his new chief of staff, Stephen Miller, to the microphone during a White House news conference. Miller proceeded to compress the achievements of an entire civilization into approximately 130 seconds of escalating presidential praise.

There were records. There were superlatives. There were military miracles, economic wonders, nuclear reactors, deportations, oil reserves, and enough historical firsts to make the Guinness Book of World Records consider filing for bankruptcy.

There was, unfortunately, also the Bureau of Labor Statistics.

And the Energy Information Administration.

And mathematics.

Those bastards.

## I. The greatest president in the history of presidents

Miller began by announcing that Trump had accomplished more in the preceding two years than any American president had ever accomplished.

Not more than Jimmy Carter.

Not more than Herbert Hoover.

More than Lincoln, Washington, Roosevelt, Eisenhower, and everybody else who ever occupied the building.

This proposition arrives with neither a definition of *accomplishment* nor a method for comparing it.

Abraham Lincoln preserved the Union and ended slavery through constitutional and military transformation. Franklin Roosevelt presided over the New Deal and much of America's mobilization during World War II.

How would either compare numerically with deregulation or a trade agreement? Miller supplied adjectives, not a metric.

History is not a television talent show. You cannot award a president eighteen points for deregulation, eleven for deportations, and a commemorative fruit basket for intimidating a reporter.

## II. The twenty-percent inflation emergency that wasn't

Here the arithmetic began trying to escape through the ventilation system.

Miller said Trump had inherited “20-plus percent year-over-year inflation” and defeated it.

The Bureau of Labor Statistics reported that in January 2025 the Consumer Price Index was **3.0 percent higher than one year earlier**.

Three.

Not twenty.

Three.

There is a defensible argument that *cumulative* inflation during the preceding administration left American households paying considerably more for groceries and other goods. But the accumulated price increase across several years is **not** the annual inflation rate Trump inherited.

This isn't an obscure distinction requiring a doctorate in econometrics. It is the difference between the price level's total increase and its rate of increase at a specified moment.

The man standing before the national press corps had collapsed the two concepts into one enormous number.

Perhaps it sounded better at twenty percent.

Unfortunately, inflation statistics are famously indifferent to enthusiasm.

[Source: BLS, CPI January 2025 release](https://www.bls.gov/news.release/archives/cpi_02122025.htm).

## III. The nuclear reactors that traveled backward through time

Miller also boasted of the “first new nuclear reactors to go critical since the 1970s.”

Now, *criticality* has a precise technical meaning: a sustained nuclear chain reaction. It is **not synonymous** with commercial operation. Any literal fact-check of his statement must identify the reactor or reactors he meant, their dates of first criticality, and the class of reactor being counted.

The broader insinuation that American reactors had not newly entered operation since the 1970s is demonstrably wrong: Watts Bar Unit 2 entered commercial service in 2016; Vogtle Unit 3 in 2023; and Vogtle Unit 4 in 2024. Those examples by themselves, however, **do not conclusively disprove** the narrower *first criticality* wording without matching technical records.

The dates deserve checking, not time travel.

Perhaps the administration has discovered an improved, politically loyal form of nuclear fission.

Or perhaps somebody should name the reactor and provide the paperwork.

[Source: U.S. Energy Information Administration, nuclear energy FAQ](https://www.eia.gov/tools/faqs/faq.php?id=228&t=6).

## IV. A brief and inconvenient encounter with an actual accomplishment

Here intellectual honesty requires a detour.

Crime fell significantly. The FBI's reported 2025 figures show a substantial decline in murder and violent crime. Those are real, favorable outcomes worthy of accurate reporting.

They should not be dismissed merely because Miller mentioned them.

But a national crime trend isn't an experiment randomly assigning presidents to identical populations. Crime had been declining before Trump took office in January 2025; state and local policy, demographic changes, reporting, and wider social conditions complicate causal attribution.

The data demonstrate the decrease. They do not, by themselves, demonstrate that one president caused the entire decrease.

This is the irritating thing about responsible journalism: occasionally the politician you're criticizing says something substantially correct, and you have to acknowledge it.

Otherwise you're operating the same machinery in reverse.

[Source: FBI Crime Data Explorer](https://cde.ucr.cjis.gov/).

## V. The magnificent Venezuelan oil multiplication machine

Miller's next miracle involved Venezuela: Trump supposedly took over the country in under an hour and doubled U.S. oil reserves overnight.

A foreign oil agreement can produce commercial interests and rights to eventual output. It does not magically relocate Venezuelan petroleum into **U.S. territorial proved reserves**. Rights, resources, commercially recoverable reserves, and barrels already produced are different categories.

Any precise doubling claim requires a starting reserves figure, a legally and geologically appropriate definition, and independently assessed recoverability.

Oil has a notoriously unpatriotic habit of requiring wells, infrastructure, financing, geology, labor, and time.

You cannot resolve those questions by shouting louder.

The rocks remain rocks.

## VI. The greatest economy ever recorded by the human species

Then Miller announced the strongest economy and fastest wage growth in American history.

Strongest by what measure?

Real GDP growth? Productivity? Employment? Median purchasing power? Inflation-adjusted pay? Investment? Income per capita?

Nominal wage increases and real wage increases are different measures. Growth over a week and growth over a year are different periods. Total output and typical household welfare are different questions.

The superlatives come without benchmarks or intervals.

An undefined superlative cannot be meaningfully tested until its author supplies the definition.

“Greatest economy ever” is not an economic indicator.

It is advertising copy.

## VII. The Ministry of Superlatives

In approximately two minutes Miller ran through supposedly complete transformations, historic firsts, whole institutions rebuilt, record production, record deportations, and a future guaranteed to exceed an already unprecedented present.

Political historians recognize techniques such as leader glorification, selective statistics, claims of national renewal, and categorical declarations that preempt close examination. Such techniques have been employed in different movements and governments; parallels are questions for careful historical analysis, not grounds to equate every government or official with Nazi Germany.

Joseph Goebbels' historical role was organizing propaganda for a genocidal Nazi dictatorship. A contemporary comparison must acknowledge that immense difference rather than use the name as a shortcut for fact-checking.

What can be examined here is Miller's actual rhetorical mechanism: independently testable propositions, disputed causal attribution, and untestable superlatives were delivered in one seamless register of absolute conviction.

That is a perfect way to conceal the boundaries between them.

## VIII. The microphone was working perfectly

The dramatic tension of Miller's appearance is the contrast between the content—unparalleled triumph—and the increasingly emphatic delivery that some viewers heard.

To supportive viewers, intensity may communicate conviction. To skeptics, it may sound like an attempt to overwhelm questions with speed and volume.

But questions are precisely what the press corps exists to ask.

Was that annual inflation or cumulative inflation?

Which reactors?

What wage series?

What qualifies as a proved reserve?

Who gets credit for falling crime?

None of these questions can be answered in decibels.

After Miller finished, Trump asked whether he wanted to run for president. The compliments had become recursive.

## IX. The thing about truth

The problem is not that every claim was false. The problem is that assertions of **very different evidentiary quality** were delivered as if equally indisputable.

A spokesperson may accurately celebrate falling crime while inaccurately describing inherited inflation. He may defend a major foreign oil arrangement while overstating its meaning. He may praise deregulation without demonstrating that no earlier president achieved a comparable result.

Serious fact-checking interrupts this process not with counter-slogans but with measures, dates, definitions, documents, independently checkable records, and corrections.

A nuclear reactor still has a criticality date.

A barrel of oil still has a location.

An inflation rate still has a numerator, a denominator, and a measurement period.

And journalists still have the right to ask officials to show their work.

You can browbeat a reporter.

You can flatter a president.

You can proclaim the greatest two years in the history of the Republic.

But you cannot intimidate the number three into becoming twenty.

Not even from the White House podium.
