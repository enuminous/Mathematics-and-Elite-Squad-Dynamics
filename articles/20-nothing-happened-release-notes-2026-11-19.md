# Nothing Happened. See Release Notes.
### The Department of Negative Results ships a build of absolutely not that.

*Mathematics and Elite Squad Dynamics · Dispatch 20 · Scientific satire · 19 November 2026*

*By Matthew Chenoweth Wright | Monolithic LLC*

We really did ship a null result with a version number.

I stared at that sentence for a while. There are worse things to have done with a Thursday. A man can spend a whole Thursday trying to make a weak effect look strong. We spent ours giving the thing that did not happen a permanent address.

## The release meeting

In the fictional offices of this journal, Professor Chaos arrived with champagne. The statistician asked him to put it back.

“Was the result inconclusive?” he asked.

“About some things.”

“Can we call it *promising*?”

“You can call the champagne promising. We haven't opened that either.”

The release manager had a different concern. If nothing happened, how were readers supposed to distinguish this nothing from the nothing in the previous release?

“That's why it has a version number,” she said. She was the only person in the room who sounded employable.

## The changelog

> **Nothing v1.0.0** *(fictional release)*  
> **Added:** A baseline with the right to win.  
> **Removed:** A sentence containing the words “clearly demonstrates.”  
> **Known issue:** The universe remains under no obligation to implement our roadmap.

The marketing department asked whether we could restore the sentence in a patch release. The statistician said yes, if the patch contained new evidence. The meeting ended much earlier than expected.

## A very particular nothing

Here is the serious part, because unlabeled bullshit is against house policy. An experiment that runs and finds no convincing effect is different from an experiment that never runs. It's also different from a test precise enough to rule out an effect of a specified size. You can print zeroes on all three reports if you're sufficiently determined to confuse everybody. You still have three different reports.

A version number lets somebody ask a useful question: *Which code, which inputs, which comparison, and which rule for calling it?* It doesn't tell them the rule was any good. It does give them something to disagree with besides your personality.

This is why I'd rather publish a dull result with a runnable record than an exciting one that requires my presence in the room. If your reproduction disagrees, now we have something to investigate. If it agrees, I can finally stop running that particular test just because I liked the hypothesis.

The journal regrets to announce that nothing will not be pivoting to enterprise.

It will, however, remain available for download.

*Editorial note: The meeting, dialogue, and software changelog are invented. The version number in the changelog is a joke, not an identifier for an actual experiment. This dispatch reports no new scientific finding.*
