### Featured mathematical atrocity: Universal Victory Principle
Let C be a nonempty candidate set and define a *partial* function

\[
V(c)=\begin{cases}
1 & \text{if candidate }c\text{ wins},\\
1 & \text{if candidate }c\text{ disputes losing}.

### Statistical reality checks
- A 95% confidence interval is a property of a repeated sampling procedure under its assumptions; it is not a 95% probability that a fixed election outcome lies inside a particular interval.
- Larger samples reduce random sampling error but do not automatically remove nonresponse, coverage, measurement, or selection bias.
- A poll's margin of error is not a guarantee against systematic error.
- A race lead is not a race call; unofficial counts are not certified results.
- Bayesian inference requires an explicit likelihood, prior, and evidence. It cannot rescue cherry-picked voters or fabricated observations.
